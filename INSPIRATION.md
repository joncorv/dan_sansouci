# Website Inspiration for Dan San Souci

## 1. Research brief

Dan San Souci's proposed site is both a working portfolio and a long-term record: an artwork-led landing page, a complete book archive (including out-of-print titles), book-detail pages, painting series and full-screen galleries, an editorial biography, and a dependable contact route. It should feel personal and painterly while remaining fast, accessible on phones, and straightforward for Dan to update in a CMS.

These references are intended to help decide:

- how much of the homepage should be immersive versus immediately navigable;
- how books, paintings, process material, and biography should relate without becoming one undifferentiated archive;
- which catalog, filtering, detail-page, gallery, and inquiry patterns will scale;
- how to preserve warmth and character without sacrificing accessibility or performance.

The goal is **not to copy any complete site**. The strongest result will combine useful ideas from several references and adapt them to Dan's work, audience, content volume, and maintenance needs.

**Websites researched:** August 19, 2026. Observations below describe what was visible or indexable on that date; recommendations are explicitly labeled as transfer ideas. Sites change over time.

### Access and research note

Eighteen listed sites were retained and two substitutions were made to keep the bank useful and verifiable. `mowillemsworkshop.com` failed over HTTPS and served an Apache default page over HTTP, so **Leo Lionni** replaces it as a child-friendly legacy reference. Nathalie Lété's site exposed little beyond a collaborator list during review, so **Quentin Blake** replaces it as a more inspectable artist-controlled archive. Brian Selznick's site returned an automated-access challenge, although its official home and indexed internal pages were discoverable; Owen Gildersleeve's home returned HTTP 406 to one direct client but was readable through another. Those limitations are noted in their entries.

## 3. Twenty researched references

> In each entry, **Observed** records the reviewed site's structure or behavior. **Transfer to Dan** is a design recommendation, not a claim about the reference site.

### 1. David Wiesner

**Live site:** <https://www.davidwiesner.com/>  
**Classification:** Close peer — children's-book author and illustrator  
**Tags:** **Books · Process · Editorial · Archive**

- **Why relevant:** Wiesner's site treats finished books, experimental work, and writing about picture-book craft as parts of one practice. That is a useful precedent for presenting Dan as more than a cover bibliography.
- **Observed character:** Restrained and artwork-centered rather than overtly child-branded. The compact structure lets project imagery and long-form text do the work.
- **Open these:** [Books](https://www.davidwiesner.com/books/), [Bio](https://www.davidwiesner.com/bio/), [Writings](https://www.davidwiesner.com/writings/), and process/project posts such as [Phone Home](https://www.davidwiesner.com/phone-home/) and [Prog](https://www.davidwiesner.com/prog/).
- **Observed architecture/UI:** Top-level areas include Books, Bio, Writings, “Odds & Ends,” and named projects. The Writings archive includes speeches, essays, and introductions; project posts provide a route from finished imagery into context and production stories.
- **Transfer to Dan:** Add a small, optional “Stories from the studio” or “Process” layer to selected book pages. A book could contain cover, spreads, sketches, and a short note without requiring a full blog.
- **Cautions:** “Odds & Ends” is personable but ambiguous as an information label. Dan's primary nav should use predictable labels. Do not let essays compete with Books and Paintings unless enough material exists to sustain them.

### 2. Brian Selznick

**Live site:** <https://brianselznick.com/>  
**Classification:** Close peer — author and illustrator  
**Tags:** **Books · Archive · Traditional · Multimedia**

- **Why relevant:** The Books hub demonstrates that a long bibliography can remain a first-class destination rather than being reduced to a few current titles.
- **Observed character:** A traditional, illustrated author site with many subject-specific branches rather than a single fashionable portfolio grid.
- **Open these:** [Books](https://brianselznick.com/books.htm), [Awards](https://brianselznick.com/awards.htm), [Movies](https://brianselznick.com/movies.htm), [Puppet Shows](https://brianselznick.com/puppet.htm), and [And More!](https://brianselznick.com/more.htm).
- **Observed architecture/UI:** The bibliography runs from recent books back through early titles, including books illustrated for other writers. Awards, adaptations, audio, ballet, events, and puppet work are separate branches. This keeps a large career record navigable without forcing all material into book records.
- **Transfer to Dan:** Default Dan's archive to newest-first chronology, show the year clearly, and include every title. Use optional related-content modules for awards, adaptations, events, or process only where they exist.
- **Cautions/access:** Automated review encountered a 403 challenge on the homepage; official internal pages were still indexed and inspectable through search results. The older, many-tab model proves depth but should not be copied visually: on mobile, Dan needs fewer top-level choices, consistent templates, and modern focus states.

### 3. Jerry Pinkney Studio

**Live site:** <https://www.justjerrypinkney.com/>  
**Classification:** Legacy/estate site for a close peer  
**Tags:** **Legacy · Books · Archive · Editorial · Paintings**

- **Why relevant:** It addresses the same central challenge Dan will eventually face: presenting a long picture-book career as a living visual legacy.
- **Observed character:** Warm, spacious, image-rich, and biographical, with watercolor work, covers, portraits, and archival photography sharing the page.
- **Open these:** the homepage's **“100 Books”** section and [Jerry's Life](https://www.justjerrypinkney.com/jerrys-life).
- **Observed architecture/UI:** A short navigation leads to a chronological 100-book presentation and a long editorial biography. “Jerry's Life” is divided by descriptive headings and alternates readable prose, pull quotes, book covers, photographs, and artwork; photographs include visible credits. Recent books and exhibitions are surfaced before the deep record.
- **Transfer to Dan:** Combine a curated homepage with a complete chronology. Structure the biography as scannable chapters—early life, first books, painting practice, career milestones, today—and store image captions/credits as real CMS fields.
- **Cautions:** Very long pages and large source images can be expensive on mobile. A complete archive should paginate or progressively load, and the biography needs an on-page contents menu if it becomes this deep. Do not allow “featured” projects to hide the full catalog.

### 4. Jan Brett

**Live site:** <https://janbrett.com/>  
**Classification:** Close peer and traditional resource-heavy author site  
**Tags:** **Books · Traditional · Playful · Resources · Archive**

- **Why relevant:** This is a strong counterexample to the idea that an author site is only a visual portfolio. It serves teachers, librarians, families, and existing fans with a very large body of useful material.
- **Observed character:** Densely decorated, characterful, and explicitly child-friendly. It prioritizes abundance over minimalism.
- **Open these:** [Book List](https://janbrett.com/booklist.htm), [Activities A–Z](https://janbrett.com/activities_pages.htm), [Coloring Pages](https://janbrett.com/activities_pages_artwork.htm), [Murals](https://janbrett.com/murals.htm), and [About Jan Brett](https://janbrett.com/biography.htm).
- **Observed architecture/UI:** The book list groups titles by role—written and illustrated, retold and illustrated, or illustrated—and supplies publisher, year, and story setting. Activities, games, media, videos, and educator resources form extensive parallel directories.
- **Transfer to Dan:** Preserve roles and credits accurately in the content model. If Dan has educator materials, make them a separate resource collection linked to relevant books rather than crowding each title page.
- **Cautions:** Hyperlink density, small controls, legacy layouts, and external jumps can overwhelm users and create mobile/accessibility problems. Borrow the completeness and audience awareness, not the old visual or navigation model.

### 5. Shaun Tan

**Live site:** <https://www.shauntan.net/>  
**Classification:** Close adjacent peer — author, illustrator, painter, and screen/theatre artist  
**Tags:** **Books · Paintings · Series · Immersive · Editorial**

- **Why relevant:** Tan's practice spans books and independent paintings without forcing one to masquerade as the other—very close to Dan's planned split.
- **Observed character:** Quiet, atmospheric, and image-led, with plain labels that keep the visual work central.
- **Open these:** Books (home), [Paintings](https://www.shauntan.net/new-page-1), [TV, film and theatre](https://www.shauntan.net/film-and-theatre), [About](https://www.shauntan.net/about), and [Contact](https://www.shauntan.net/contact-1). The Paintings page introduces bodies of work—“Go, Said the Bird,” “Small Paintings,” and “Paintings 2000–2010”—with context, medium/size information, and links to larger galleries.
- **Transfer to Dan:** Give each painting category a representative image, a short curatorial introduction, and a “View gallery” route. Use one shared artwork record underneath all category and lightbox views.
- **Cautions:** Several page labels use generic generated slugs, and the relationship between overview and gallery is not always obvious from the URL. Dan's CMS should create human-readable URLs and stable deep links.

### 6. Carson Ellis

**Live site:** <https://www.carsonellis.com/>  
**Classification:** Adjacent illustrator, author, and artist  
**Tags:** **Illustration · Paintings · Eclectic · Grid**

- **Why relevant:** Ellis's work index mixes book imagery, commissions, personal paintings, textiles, and objects while retaining concise attribution.
- **Observed character:** An eclectic image wall—art-led and informal rather than separated into many institutional categories.
- **Open these:** [Work/home](https://www.carsonellis.com/), [About](https://www.carsonellis.com/about), and [Workshops](https://www.carsonellis.com/workshops).
- **Observed architecture/UI:** The primary nav is only Work, About, Workshops, and Shop. The Work page pairs images with compact captions such as title, medium or role, client/publisher, and year. Examples include book studies, gouache paintings, album art, quilts, and editorial illustration.

- **Cautions:** A single mixed wall becomes hard to browse when users arrive with a specific task. Dan still needs dedicated Books and Paintings indexes, and repeated images from one project should be grouped instead of appearing as unrelated records.

### 7. Isabelle Arsenault

**Live site:** <https://www.isabellearsenault.com/>  
**Classification:** Close peer — children's-book illustrator  
**Tags:** **Books · Book details · Illustration · Quiet**

- **Why relevant:** The site offers a clear sequence from a discipline landing page, to a book-cover grid, to generous title-specific interior art.
- **Observed character:** Restrained, elegant, and light, with generous image space and little visual competition from the interface.
- **Open these:** [Books](https://www.isabellearsenault.com/books), [Illustration](https://www.isabellearsenault.com/illustration), and the detail page for [The Mouse Who Carried a House on His Back](https://www.isabellearsenault.com/books/mouse).
- **Observed architecture/UI:** Books and Illustration are separate hubs. The Books grid links to stable title pages. The Mouse page includes creator credits, publisher/year, a quoted review, several interior spreads, a cover/publisher link, and an editions list by language and publisher.
- **Transfer to Dan:** Make interior spreads the visual reward on each detail page and keep bibliographic data close to the title. Model editions independently so translations, reprints, or formats can be added without duplicating the book.
- **Cautions:** Beautifully sparse title pages can omit practical discovery data such as ISBN, reading range, availability, or library links. Dan's details should retain the calm appearance while exposing complete metadata.

### 8. Christian Robinson — The Art of Fun

- **Cautions:** External-link-heavy features weaken continuity and can leave users unsure where they are going. Label external destinations and do not use the homepage as a substitute for Dan's complete catalog.

## 5. Synthesis for Dan's planned pages

### Homepage

- **Full-bleed artwork:** Use one work per viewport, chosen from a CMS-managed featured set of roughly four to six. Do not collage multiple paintings over one another. Preserve a dependable text-safe region for Dan's name and navigation.
- **Ken Burns-style movement:** If used, apply a very slow pan/scale to a high-resolution derivative—not the archival original—and avoid simultaneous crossfade and camera movement. A 12–20 second duration should feel like looking, not advertising.
- **Static alternative:** The site must remain complete with motion off. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, show a static hero (or an intentional, user-controlled crossfade with no autoplay). A CMS toggle should also allow Dan to disable motion globally.
- **Artwork captions:** Keep title, year, medium, and “View painting” available in a quiet caption. Do not bake these into the image. Allow the caption to collapse visually while remaining reachable.
- **Navigation:** Dan's name/logo and Books, Paintings, About, Contact must remain visible or available through a clearly labeled Menu button. Contrast should come from a solid/translucent backing or adaptive theme selected per hero—not an unreliable text shadow.
- **Continuation cue:** Add a textual “Explore Dan's work” or downward arrow with an accessible label. The next section should peek into the first viewport on short screens; never require users to guess that the page scrolls.
- **Mobile cropping:** Store a focal point and optional portrait crop for each hero. Preview phone, tablet, and desktop crops in the CMS. Prefer `<picture>` art direction when one crop cannot protect the subject.
- **Rotation:** Avoid a fast automatic carousel. If artwork changes, pause on hover/focus, provide pause and previous/next buttons, and do not unexpectedly move keyboard focus.

### Books index

- **Default view:** A responsive cover grid with visible title and year. Use consistent image boxes without stretching covers; subtle shadows are optional and should not be the only boundary.
- **Browsing order:** Default to **newest first**, with controls for **oldest first**, **title A–Z**, and possibly **title Z–A**. Chronology communicates a career; alphabetical order helps someone find a known title.
- **Filters:** Start with only useful facets: Dan's role (author/illustrator/both), decade, series, and availability (in print/out of print). Add age/format only if data is reliable across the archive.
- **Featured versus complete:** A short “Selected/Featured books” row may lead the page, but the complete archive must follow immediately and state its count. “Featured” is a CMS flag, not a duplicate collection.
- **Out-of-print titles:** Include them with a neutral **Out of print** badge and library/WorldCat or publisher-record links where available. Never remove a title merely because it cannot be purchased.
- **Search:** Search title, collaborator, ISBN, publisher, series, and keyword. Show the query, number of results, active filters, a reset option, and a helpful zero-results message.
- **CMS operation:** One `Book` record should generate the card, detail page, search result, related-book links, and homepage feature. Enforce required title, slug, primary cover, year, role, and status; allow drafts for incomplete historical records.
- **Scale/performance:** Paginate or use a “Load more” button with real paginated URLs. Do not infinite-scroll without preserving browser history and footer access.

### Book details

Each page should use a consistent, structured metadata block:

- **Title** and optional subtitle;
- **Author and illustrator credits** as separate repeatable people/role fields;
- **Original publication date** (and edition-specific dates where needed);
- **Publisher/imprint**;
- **ISBN-10/ISBN-13** per edition where available;
- **Synopsis** in Dan's or the publisher's approved text;
- **Cover image** with accessible alt text;
- **Interior artwork** as an ordered gallery with captions where meaningful;
- **Awards and selected review excerpts**, including source and link;
- **Intended age, grade, or reading range**, only where authoritative;
- **Editions/formats/languages** (hardcover, paperback, ebook, reissue, translation);
- **Availability status** including out of print;
- **Purchase, publisher, independent-bookstore, and library links**, clearly marked as external;
- **Related books** by series, collaborator, subject, or editorial selection.

Layout recommendation: title/credits and cover first; synopsis and key metadata second; interior spreads as the visual centerpiece; awards, editions, links, and related books afterward. Include breadcrumbs or “Back to all books,” but preserve the user's filters and scroll position when they return.

### Paintings and painting categories

- **Category landing:** The Paintings index should show one strong representative image, category name, short descriptor, and work count for each series/category.
- **Series structure:** A category page should contain a title, 40–100 word introduction, optional hero, and gallery generated from artwork relationships—not a manually assembled page.
- **Grid versus masonry:** Prefer a regular grid when comparisons and orderly scanning matter. Masonry respects mixed proportions but creates a less predictable reading order. A justified-row gallery can preserve aspect ratio while maintaining clear rows; test it carefully with keyboard order.
- **Artwork metadata:** Store title, year, medium, dimensions (height × width × depth with units), series, caption/notes, credit/copyright, primary image, optional detail images, alt text, focal point, display priority, and availability/location.
- **Full-screen viewer:** Open into a dialog-like viewer with a visible close button, caption toggle, previous/next controls, position (“7 of 24”), and zoom only if the source resolution justifies it. Do not trap users in a dead-end overlay.
- **Inputs:** Support Left/Right arrows, Escape to close, Tab through controls, swipe on touch, and labeled on-screen buttons. Return focus to the originating thumbnail on close.
- **Deep links:** Every work should have a canonical URL. Opening a lightbox may update the URL so a work can be shared; a non-JavaScript visit to that URL should still render a complete artwork page.
- **Inquiry status:** Optional statuses can be `Not for sale`, `Available`, `On hold`, `Sold`, `Private collection`, or `Inquire`. Hide price unless Dan wants public sales. “Inquire” should prefill the artwork title and inventory ID.

### Biography

- **Editorial typography:** Use a comfortable serif for body text, approximately 65–75 characters per line, with strong subheads and restrained display type. Avoid tiny all-caps for long labels.
- **Story structure:** Break the biography into meaningful sections instead of one unbroken block. Consider a brief introduction followed by early life, books, painting practice, selected milestones, and current work.
- **Archival imagery:** Alternate portraits, studio photographs, sketches, historic covers, and ephemera with prose. Every image needs date/context where known and a photographer/source credit.
- **Timeline:** Use a selective timeline for major publications, awards, exhibitions, and career turns—not every book. The complete bibliography remains in Books.
- **Awards/exhibitions:** Store each item as structured data (name, organization/venue, work, year, link) so selected entries can appear in Bio and full lists can be added later.
- **Balance:** Begin with a short readable biography; put extended history lower down or behind a clear “Full biography” link. Images should illuminate adjacent text rather than interrupt every paragraph.

### Contact

- **Form:** Ask only for name, email, inquiry type, message, and optional organization. Avoid phone/address unless needed.
- **Routing:** Offer Publishing/rights, School or speaking visit, Original art/sales, Press/media, Website issue, and General. Each selection can route internally without exposing private addresses.
- **Context:** Artwork and book inquiry buttons should pass a hidden record ID and display the referenced title to the sender.
- **Spam:** Use a honeypot, rate limiting, time-to-submit checks, and server-side validation. Add a privacy-respecting challenge only if abuse requires it.
- **States:** Provide inline field errors, an error summary, a disabled/sending state that does not erase text, and a clear success receipt. If submission fails, retain the entered message and offer a fallback email link.
- **Reliability:** Deliver through a transactional email service, send Dan and the visitor a confirmation, log only necessary metadata, and test the production form regularly.

## 7. Accessibility and maintainability

### Accessibility checklist

- **Image alt text:** Write alt text for the image's purpose in context. A book cover might be “Cover of *The Talking Eggs*, illustrated by Dan San Souci”; a painting may need a concise description of subject, setting, and mood. Decorative textures should use empty alt text. Do not auto-fill alt text from a filename.
- **Artwork captions:** Keep title, year, medium, dimensions, and credit in adjacent HTML text. Captions and alt text are complementary: the caption identifies the object; alt text conveys relevant visual content.
- **Galleries:** Use buttons for open/close/previous/next, announce slide changes politely, expose current position, support Escape and arrow keys, and restore focus to the thumbnail. Swipe cannot be the only control.
- **Focus:** Use a clearly visible focus ring with sufficient contrast on both pale and dark artwork. Never remove outlines without an equally visible replacement.
- **Motion:** Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`; stop automatic pans, crossfades, smooth scrolling, animated thumbnails, and parallax. Provide a visible pause control for any motion lasting more than a few seconds.
- **Contrast over art:** Put navigation/captions on a solid or sufficiently opaque surface. Do not assume a gradient works for every crop. Test each CMS-selectable light/dark treatment.
- **Mobile crops:** Store focal points/alternate crops, preserve the subject, and avoid placing essential information inside images. Test 320px width and landscape phones.
- **Book grids:** Use responsive columns—often two on narrow phones, three or four on tablets, more on wide screens—with adequate gaps and 44×44px minimum interactive targets.
- **Semantics:** One logical `h1` per page, sequential headings, landmark regions, skip link, descriptive link text, language metadata, and real lists for archives.
- **Forms:** Every field needs a visible label; errors must be associated with fields and summarized; success/failure cannot rely on color alone.

### Performance checklist

- Generate AVIF/WebP plus JPEG fallbacks and responsive widths; never serve the archival master directly.
- Reserve image dimensions/aspect ratios to prevent layout shift.
- Eager-load only the first meaningful hero; lazy-load below-the-fold covers and gallery images.
- Use a lightweight blurred/color placeholder, not dozens of full-size hidden slideshow images.
- Prefer CSS transitions over animation libraries for simple fades. Avoid autoplay video backgrounds and ubiquitous GIF thumbnails.
- Load full lightbox resolution only after the viewer opens it; use zoom tiles only if genuinely needed.
- Subset/self-host fonts, keep type families/weights limited, and ensure text remains visible during font loading.
- Monitor representative archive, category, book-detail, and mobile homepage pages—not only the homepage—with performance budgets.

### CMS-friendly content model

| Content type | Core fields/relationships |
|---|---|
| **Book** | Title, slug, subtitle, synopsis, original date, role, credits, cover, ordered interior images, subjects, series, awards/reviews, featured rank, related books |
| **Edition** | Parent book, publisher/imprint, date, ISBN, format, language, region, availability, purchase/publisher/library links |
| **Artwork** | Title, slug/inventory ID, year, medium, dimensions, series/categories, images/details, description, alt text, focal point, availability/location, featured rank |
| **Painting category/series** | Name, slug, introduction, representative/hero work, display order, related artworks |
| **Person/organization** | Name, role, URL; reusable for authors, publishers, photographers, venues, reviewers |
| **Award/review** | Type, name/quote, source, year, link, related book/artwork |
| **Exhibition/milestone** | Title, venue/organization, location, dates/year, description, images, related artworks |
| **Media asset** | Image/file, alt text, caption, creator/credit, copyright/source, focal point, usage notes |
| **Site settings** | Contact routing, social links, homepage selection, hero timing/motion toggle, default SEO/share image |

### Keeping the archive manageable

1. Create fixed editorial rules for titles, dates, dimensions, role labels, and out-of-print status.
2. Import the initial bibliography from a spreadsheet into structured records; do not manually build cards in a page editor.
3. Use validation and controlled dropdowns for series, roles, availability, languages, and formats.
4. Allow incomplete historical records to remain drafts; display “Information unavailable” only when helpful, not empty labels.
5. Add “last reviewed” internally to links and availability, with a periodic broken-link report.
6. Derive index cards, related items, feeds, and sitemaps automatically from records.
7. Give Dan preview, draft, crop/focal-point, reorder, and rollback tools; restrict layout changes to safe components.
8. Document a five-minute workflow for adding a book, painting, category, and homepage feature.
